For the flight in front of you

  • The chart, the route, the airspace

    The current FAA sectional, terminal area and IFR enroute charts. Type two airports and the course is drawn on the chart, with the Class B, C and D airspace, TFRs and special-use airspace along it.

  • A Brief that starts with the answer

    Go, caution or no-go first, then at most three things to do about it: the METARs and TAFs along the way, the hazards on each leg, and a briefing in plain words.

  • A nav log from the winds aloft

    Headings, groundspeeds, times and fuel for each leg from the forecast winds, and a cruising altitude picked for terrain, airspace and the clouds.

  • Every airport's card

    Runways, frequencies, lighting and the weather there now, with the D-ATIS and runway visual range where the field has them.

  • Charts kept for the air

    Download the charts and data for a region before you go, and the map works with no signal.

  • In the cockpit

    What ATC says, turned into words on your phone, a clearance's route entered for you to check, and a logbook entry drafted from the flight you flew.
